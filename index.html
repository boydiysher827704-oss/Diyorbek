01 · CONTEXT

Why this exists

PROBLEM / USER

Manual railway track inspection is slow, expensive, and prone to human error. Intended user: a track inspection team using this as a screening aid, not an automated pass/fail authority.

HOW IT WORKS

  • Inspector uploads a photo of a rail section.
  • A fine-tuned YOLOv8 model scans it for defect patterns.
  • App returns a label + confidence score for human review.

WHY RECALL FIRST

Missing a real defect (false negative) is costlier than a false alarm. The model — and its evaluation — prioritize catching real defects over avoiding false alarms.

05 · DISCLOSED LIMITATION

What the numbers don't fully show

⚠ Confirmed near-duplicate leakage

12.5%

A perceptual-hash check comparing every test image against every training image found that 14 of 112 test images have a near-identical match in the training set — 13 of those pairs were exact perceptual duplicates.

This means the test metrics above are likely somewhat optimistic. Full methodology in ISSUE_LOG.md and EDA_NOTES.md.

06 · WHAT'S NEXT

Future direction: camera + laser + AI

A concept roadmap, not a built product. Framed around how real rail networks already inspect track today.

● Stage 1 — Today

Camera + AI/ML

Single-photo defect screening using the YOLOv8 model in this repo. Manual capture, human-in-the-loop review.

Stage 2 — Planned

+ Laser profiling

Add a laser line/triangulation scanner alongside the camera to capture 3D rail profile — the same core approach used in real systems like Pavemetrics LRAIL and ENSCO's rail measurement tools.

Stage 3 — Planned

Vehicle-mounted inspection

Mount camera + laser + model on an inspection trolley or defectoscope car for continuous scanning instead of single photos.

Real-world rail inspection vehicle ("ВАГОН-ДЕФЕКТОСКОП") — reference photo of existing industry equipment, not this project's own hardware.

Vehicles like this already carry sensors along the track. Combining a laser profiler (for precise 3D geometry) with a camera-based AI model (for visual defect classification) is a well-established pattern in industrial rail inspection — it's not a new idea, but it's a credible, grounded next step for this project.

The camera+AI part of that pipeline is what this Capstone builds and evaluates today. The laser-scanning and vehicle-mounted parts are the natural next stages, not yet implemented.

Reference systems: Pavemetrics LRAIL, ENSCO rail measurement systems, laser triangulation scanning research.
